# Manuscript Argument Integrity Protocol

Apply at Stage 11 writing and Stage 12 manuscript review. Write **claim-forward, evidence-bounded** prose: do not weaken a well-supported claim merely to anticipate criticism, and do not hide, demote, redefine, or replace scientifically relevant evidence because it is unfavorable. Preserve **Paper Scope ≤ Identified Claim ≤ Evidence**, the frozen Scope Contract, no significance hunting, and no complexity rescue. This protocol does not authorize a new research question, primary outcome/evidence, Claim Type, Evidence Architecture, causal design, or Gate decision; route substantive design changes through the existing scope-change and escalation rules.

## 1. Argument-first architecture

Organize the reader's path as **Scientific Question → Gap → Claim → Evidence → Interpretation → Contribution**. Prefer this argument to a chronology of attempted analyses. Include research history, failed attempts, and deviations when needed for methodological transparency, identification, preregistration, reproducibility, or honest interpretation; do not erase them to sharpen the story.

## 2. Section and paragraph contract

Before substantive drafting or revision of a major section or paragraph, state briefly:

- **FUNCTION:** what the reader must learn at this point;
- **CLAIM:** its narrowest defensible main point;
- **AUTHORIZED EVIDENCE:** the verified result, table, figure, citation, or institutional fact that supports it;
- **INFERENCE LIMIT:** the stronger inference the evidence does not establish;
- **BRIDGE:** how it advances the next part of the argument.

Keep the contract lightweight; it is an audit aid, not a new form required for every sentence. If the claim lacks authorized evidence, obtain evidence, narrow the claim, or mark the gap unresolved. Fluency cannot repair that gap.

## 3. Two-way alignment

- **Top-down:** Core Question → Claim → Method / Design → Required Evidence → Observed Evidence.
- **Bottom-up:** Observed Evidence → Result → Interpretation → Contribution → Conclusion → Abstract → Title.

Repair any broken link or narrow the claim. The manuscript must not turn a descriptive, measurement, spatial, or associational finding into a causal one; causal claims retain the complete identification standard in `core/PRINCIPLES.md`.

## 4. Anti-defensive boundary

Remove empty hedging, repeated caveats, ritual self-criticism, vague apologies, and statements about absent but irrelevant analyses when they add no precision. Replace them with a direct, scoped statement of what is identified, for which population/setting/design, with what uncertainty, and what remains untested. Do not weaken a supported contribution merely to pre-answer hypothetical criticism.

Retain real identification and measurement limits, uncertainty, sample support, external-validity boundaries, unresolved rivals, and scientifically relevant null, negative, unexpected, or contradictory results. State important limits wherever the reader needs them to understand a claim, including an abstract or conclusion when material. A cleaner sentence may not change scientific meaning. Never use anti-defensive editing to suppress evidence, hide a weak design, or recast post-hoc analysis as confirmatory.

## 5. Evidence role and result integrity

Give each main table, figure, and key robustness or mechanism test an explicit job: establish primary evidence, validate a construct, address a named threat, distinguish a rival, test a mechanism, or bound external validity. Remove redundant display, not necessary unfavorable evidence. Do not switch primary outcomes, samples, treatments, tests, evaluation dimensions, or the central story because of significance or an inconvenient result. If results challenge the frozen claim, report the conflict and use the existing scope-change/escalation process before any substantive redesign.

## 6. Change-impact and synchronization

Classify each edit as **SEMANTIC / CLAIM**, **EVIDENCE / RESULT**, **METADATA**, or **SURFACE LANGUAGE**. Only a genuinely surface-language edit can remain local. For a substantive claim or evidence change, audit every affected item: Title, Abstract, Introduction, Theory / Hypotheses, Institution / Background, Data, Methods / Empirical Strategy, Results, Robustness, Mechanism, Discussion, Conclusion, Tables, Figures, Appendix / Supplement, and submission materials. For metadata changes, reconcile every place that repeats the affected fact. Record unresolved inconsistencies; do not silently normalize them.

## 7. Exact-deliverable check

Rebuild Introduction, Abstract, and Title from the stabilized full evidence system rather than patching a proposal-era narrative. Audit the exact manuscript and companion files intended for submission, not an earlier draft. After any material edit, rerun the affected claim–evidence, number, citation, cross-section, and submission-package consistency checks. An unresolved blocking mismatch prevents a submission recommendation.
